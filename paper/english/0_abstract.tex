\begin{abstract}
Fruit yield estimation is critical for orchard management, harvest scheduling, and market planning. Traditional manual estimation methods suffer from subjectivity and errors typically ranging from 30\% to 50\%. The emergence of consumer-grade LiDAR sensors embedded in devices such as the iPad Pro and iPhone Pro series presents new opportunities for low-cost, portable three-dimensional data collection in agricultural settings. This paper presents the design and implementation of FruitTreeScanner, a system that leverages the LiDAR sensor on consumer devices combined with CoreML-based image detection for fruit 3D reconstruction, detection, and yield estimation. The system employs a multi-modal fusion strategy combining 2D visual detection with 3D point cloud clustering, an adaptive DBSCAN clustering algorithm, an occlusion correction model, and a dual-route yield estimation approach. Experimental validation on simulated data demonstrates that the system can effectively process point cloud data and identify fruit candidates, though real-field validation remains pending. This work provides a feasible framework for applying consumer-grade LiDAR technology to precision agriculture fruit yield estimation.
\end{abstract}
